The Unbearable Silence: Solitude as the Defining Condition of Modernity in Modernist Drama

By Rian Mistry • August 2025

Modernist drama, emerging from the seismic shifts of the late 19th and early 20th centuries – industrialization, world wars, the collapse of traditional belief systems, and the rise of existential philosophy – relentlessly probed the human condition under modernity. At its core, as illuminated by seminal playwrights Henrik Ibsen, Bertolt Brecht, Samuel Beckett, and Eugène Ionesco, lies a profound exploration of solitude. This essay argues that across diverse dramatic styles – realism, epic theatre, and the absurd – solitude is consistently depicted not merely as an occasional state, but as the inescapable, defining condition of modern existence, manifesting in psychological, socio-political, and existential forms, and serving as a crucible for truth, alienation, and the ultimate absurdity of human connection.
